# Step 7: local boundary refinement without additional possessions

Research experiment, not promoted. The Step 6 duration candidate remains intact, with its recorded public spellF1 of 0.557143. This Step 7 experiment was not scored on the public sample because it failed its development gate.

## What was tested

An opt-in post-decoding rule searches within one second of each existing H/A switch. It requires a clean sequence of original confirmed control from the old team followed by the new team. Conflicting confirmed contacts, period/frame discontinuities, out-of-bounds observations or D weight above neutral veto the proposal. D boundaries are not moved. Neighboring proposals have disjoint movement budgets to avoid crossing boundaries or deleting a short possession.

The proposed handover is the midpoint between the last old-team supported frame and the first new-team supported frame. **This is a fixed uncertainty-bracket heuristic, not an estimated optimum or a guaranteed controlled-reception instant.** In particular, when possession should stay with the previous team during a loose ball, the midpoint may be earlier than a genuine controlled takeover. This assumption is one reason the approach may not help; aggregate results do not establish the cause of each error. No threshold or anchor was changed after looking at the results.

Refinement operates on decoded labels before the existing X shield. It does not change ownership emissions, model posteriors, entropy, D evidence or duration parameters. Reported probabilities remain those of the underlying sequence model, not recalibrated confidence for the adjusted boundary. The local rule cannot recover a completely absent opponent possession or a missed stoppage.

## Four-way development comparison

| Model | Mean partial spellF1 | Mean H/A macroF1 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Original | 0.459170 | 0.785525 |
| Original + refinement | 0.461305 | 0.785669 |
| Step 6 duration model | **0.509190** | **0.793171** |
| Duration + refinement | 0.507620 | 0.792297 |

The primary comparison, declared before evaluation, was duration + refinement versus duration. Mean spellF1 decreased by 0.001570, with wins on only 3/12 matches and paired match-bootstrap 90% interval [-0.002467, -0.000703]. The standalone ablation improved the original by 0.002135, wins on 8/12, interval [0.000781, 0.003534], but failed the predeclared minimum gain of 0.01. Neither result passed its development gate; the standalone ablation was not substituted for the declared primary comparison.

The duration parameters were estimated leave-one-match-out, using only the other eleven development matches' uncensored interior reference spells. No scored match fitted its own duration scale. These remain repeatedly inspected partial automatic H/A development references without D targets, not a new independent holdout. Whole-match bootstrap intervals do not cover provider errors, domain shift or repeated model development. The eight reused comparison matches and public labels were not evaluated in this step.

Full-play label sequences were preserved on every development match. Partial-reference spell counts can still change when a moved boundary crosses an unknown reference interval; the invariant is the full-play sequence, evaluated independently of GT gaps. Refinement changed an average of 71.25 frames per original-model match and 61.08 frames per duration-model match. Transition F1 within one second moved 0.202508 to 0.205585 on the original and 0.289479 to 0.289134 on the duration model.
